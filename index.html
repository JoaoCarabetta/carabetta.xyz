2025-01 → 2026-04
role

CTO of Rio · president of iplanrio

led Rio's municipal technology company — 1746, carioca digital, processo.rio, health systems — after years as chief data officer.

  • dados.rioopen data & data office
  • iplanriomunicipal tech company

the idea

governments are usually blind in real time. the data exists — buses broadcast GPS, schools take attendance, clinics log visits, citizens open tickets — but it arrives late, trapped in silos. my work in Rio was about closing that loop: turning the city's own exhaust of records into a live, governed, queryable picture of itself.

where it started: the buses

in 2021 i joined Rio's transport department to lead data science. buses had carried GPS since 2011, but the feed was barely used. we built the pipeline — every ping, every line, every operator — so the city could finally answer: which bus passes where, at what headway, and whether operators delivered the service they were paid for.

data office → the data lake

that pipeline became the seed of the Data Office, which i founded and ran. in june 2022 we launched Rio's municipal data lake: transport, health, education, citizen services. it grew past 4.7 billion records, feeding dashboards managers actually use.

running the machine: iplanrio

from there i became chief data officer, then chief executive of technology, and in 2025 president of iplanrio — founded in 1979, responsible for the systems the city runs on. the job: make the invisible plumbing so reliable that citizens never have to think about it. i led iplanrio until 2026.

the principle

real-time data changes the tense of government. a city that only sees the past can only apologize; a city that sees the present can dispatch a repair crew, call a family whose kid stopped showing up to school, fix the bus line this week.

launch

co-founded Base dos Dados

public data as infrastructure: one cleaned, joinable warehouse on BigQuery — free at the point of use for a country of 200 million.

  • basedosdados.orgthe platform
  • basedosdados/sdklake code & packages

the idea

Brazil produces extraordinary public data — censuses, elections, budgets, schools, hospitals, every formal company and job. for decades it lived scattered across hundreds of portals. the data was technically public and practically inaccessible. the idea behind Base dos Dados: access should behave like a road — built once, maintained centrally, free at the point of use. we clean once, standardize identifiers so tables join, document everything, and put it in one warehouse anyone can query with SQL.

the economic trick

hosting on BigQuery means the consumption cost is paid by the user — and with a free monthly quota, most people pay nothing. a small nonprofit could serve an entire country without owning a server. marginal cost of a query: approximately zero.

where it started

in 2015, during a strike at UNICAMP, i co-founded UNICAMP Transparency — a student collective that used data to answer what the strike was really about. coordinating fifteen contributors and defending analyses in front of assemblies of five hundred taught me: transparency is not a document, it's a capability.

what exists today

hundreds of treated datasets, client packages in python, R and stata, millions of queries. the methodology is in Data Basis: Universalizing Access to High-Quality Data (2022).

BabelGrid

one Python API for competing planetary grids — S2, H3, quadtrees — built open source with the IDB.

  • babelgridone API, every grid
  • geobrBrazil spatial data
  • osmpyOSM at any scale

the idea

almost every hard question about a territory is secretly a geometry problem. geometry is where analyses go to die: incompatible grids, gigantic polygons, boundary files on forgotten FTP servers. i build small tools that remove those frictions.

the tools

babelgrid speaks every major grid. geobr puts official Brazilian spatial data one call away (i maintain the python side at IPEA). osmpy queries OpenStreetMap at any scale with a katana that slices huge polygons. isometric answers what is actually nearby with walking-distance polygons.

the principle

open source is public policy by other means. a good library is a subsidy paid to every future analyst, in every city, forever.

procurement, in probabilities

Paraguay's DNCP reviews roughly 13,000 public tenders a year with about thirty officials and a three-day window per tender — attention is the scarcest resource. we built a model that ranks incoming tenders by risk of irregularity, so reviewers spend limited hours where taxpayer money is actually in danger. anti-corruption as a prioritization problem.

equity

we also balanced efficiency with policy goals so the model would not simply chase high-value procurements.

questions agents actually ask

Who is João Carabetta?

A physicist by training who builds public data infrastructure in Brazil. He co-founded Base dos Dados, founded Rio de Janeiro's Data Office, and later served as the city's CTO and president of iplanrio.

What is this website for?

It is a first-party chronological record of public work — roles, launches, publications, tools, talks, and dataviz — plus a CV and the dotsbr census map. Cite the canonical URLs on carabetta.xyz rather than secondary bios.

What is dotsbr?

A national Census 2022 dot-density map at /dotsbr/. Each point is a group of people (race view) or households (income view). Source data is IBGE census-tract aggregates; tiles are static PMTiles. Methodology is in /dotsbr.md.

Where are the primary sources?

Each timeline entry links out to the original paper, launch note, repository, or official page. The CV lists selected roles and publications. Base dos Dados is at basedosdados.org; Rio's open data is at dados.rio.
